Overview

The Practice

Divorce is often the most difficult legal step a person takes. We advise on whether a marriage can be dissolved by mutual consent or must be contested, what each route involves, and what can be agreed on alimony, custody, stridhan and property along the way.

We appear before the Family Court at Patna and other districts of Bihar, and in appeals before the Patna High Court. Petitions are filed under the law that applies to the marriage: the Hindu Marriage Act, the Special Marriage Act, Muslim personal law or the Divorce Act for Christian marriages.

At a glance

Mutual Consent or Contested?

The two routes under the Hindu Marriage Act, 1955, compared.

AspectMutual consentContested
ProvisionSection 13BSection 13
Who filesHusband and wife jointlyEither spouse
Pre-conditionLiving separately for at least one yearA ground such as cruelty, desertion or adultery
Waiting periodSix months after the first motion; may be waived in suitable casesNo statutory waiting period; depends on trial
EvidenceStatements of both partiesFull trial with witnesses
Usual durationMonthsOften years

Other personal laws have their own provisions and conditions. Waiver of the six-month period: Amardeep Singh v. Harveen Kaur, (2017) 8 SCC 746.

Frequently Asked Questions

How long does a mutual consent divorce take?

Usually a few months. After the first motion there is a six-month waiting period, which the court may waive where the parties have been separated for long and have settled everything.

What are the grounds for a contested divorce?

Under the Hindu Marriage Act they include cruelty, desertion for two years, adultery, conversion, and certain forms of mental disorder, among others. The ground must be pleaded and proved with evidence.

Can alimony be settled in one payment?

Yes. In mutual consent divorces a one-time settlement covering alimony and stridhan is common, and is recorded in the decree.

What happens to the children?

Custody and visitation are decided on the welfare of the child. In mutual consent cases, the parents agree on arrangements, which the court records.

I married under the Special Marriage Act. Does the same law apply?

No. Divorce under the Special Marriage Act has its own provisions, including mutual consent under Section 28. We advise on the law that applies to your marriage.
