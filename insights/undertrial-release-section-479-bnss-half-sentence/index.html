Undertrial in Jail for Too Long? Release under Section 479 BNSS, Explained Simply

By Pankaj Kumar Choudhary, Advocate, Patna High Court · Choudhary Law Chambers, Patna · 19 November 2026

Many people in Bihar's jails are undertrials: their case has not been decided, yet they have been in custody for a long time. The law sets an outer limit. This guide explains Section 479 of the Bharatiya Nagarik Suraksha Sanhita, 2023 (BNSS) in simple words.

In this article

  3. Who is not covered

First, learn these words

  • Undertrial: a person in custody whose investigation, inquiry or trial is still going on.
  • Maximum sentence: the highest punishment the law prescribes for the offence charged.
  • First-time offender: a person who has never been convicted of any offence in the past.
  • Bond: a written undertaking to appear in court, with or without sureties.

What Section 479 says

The Ministry of Home Affairs quoted the section in its letters to all States and Union Territories (16 October 2024 and 1 January 2025) asking them to implement it. In simple words:

  • Half the maximum sentence: an undertrial who has been in detention for up to one-half of the maximum imprisonment for the offence "shall be released by the Court on bail".
  • One-third for a first-time offender: a first-time offender is to be released on bond after detention up to one-third of the maximum imprisonment.
  • Jail superintendent's duty: on completion of one-half or one-third, the Superintendent of the jail must "forthwith make an application in writing" to the court for release.

Section 479 replaced Section 436A of the old CrPC, as shown in the CrPC–BNSS comparison table of the Government of Tripura. The first-time offender rule and the superintendent's duty are new.

Who is not covered

  • Offences for which death or life imprisonment is one of the punishments.
  • Under Section 479(2), a person against whom investigation, inquiry or trial in more than one offence or in multiple cases is pending.
  • The court, after hearing the Public Prosecutor, may order continued detention for reasons recorded in writing.

How to calculate

Take the maximum punishment for the offence in the FIR or chargesheet. Divide by two (or by three for a first-time offender). Compare it with the total time already spent in custody in that case. The custody certificate from the jail gives the exact period.

An imaginary example

This is an imaginary example to explain the calculation; it is not a real case.

Manoj (an imaginary name) has been in jail in Bhagalpur for 2 years and 8 months in a case where the maximum punishment is 7 years. He has no earlier conviction and no other pending case. One-third of 7 years is 2 years and 4 months, which he has already crossed. His family asks the jail superintendent whether the application under Section 479(3) has been sent, and an application is also moved in the trial court with the custody certificate.

If release does not happen

If the trial court refuses, or the application is not decided, a petition can be filed in the Patna High Court. See also bail in the Patna High Court after rejection.

Documents to keep ready

  • Custody certificate from the jail (total period in custody)
  • FIR and chargesheet (to show the section and its maximum punishment)
  • Proof of no earlier conviction, if claiming first-time offender
  • Copies of orders in the case (see eCourts Services)

Frequently asked questions

How long must an undertrial be in jail before Section 479 applies?

Up to one-half of the maximum sentence for the offence; up to one-third for a first-time offender.

Does it apply in murder or rape cases?

Not where death or life imprisonment is one of the punishments for the offence.

Who has to apply?

The jail superintendent must apply to the court on completion of the period. The accused or family can also move an application.

What if there are several cases against the person?

Under Section 479(2), a person with more than one offence or multiple cases pending is not released under this section.
