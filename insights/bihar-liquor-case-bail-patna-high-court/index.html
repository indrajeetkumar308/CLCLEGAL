Bail in Bihar Liquor Cases: Prohibition Act, Special Court and the Patna High Court

By Pankaj Kumar Choudhary, Advocate, Patna High Court · Choudhary Law Chambers, Patna · 4 November 2026

Bihar has prohibited liquor since 2016, and cases under the Bihar Prohibition and Excise Act, 2016 make up a large share of bail matters in the courts of Bihar. The law is strict, but the route to bail depends on what the case is about: drinking, or possession, transport and sale. Knowing which provision applies decides where you apply and what you argue.

Two very different kinds of case

1. Drinking or being found drunk (Section 37)

After the Bihar Prohibition and Excise (Amendment) Act, 2022, a person found drinking or drunk is produced before an Executive Magistrate and released on paying a penalty notified by the State Government. If the penalty is not paid, the person faces one month's simple imprisonment. These cases are decided by summary trial. For repeat offences the State Government can prescribe further penalty or imprisonment by notification. A first offence under Section 37(1) is bailable under Section 76(1).

2. Manufacture, possession, transport or sale (Section 30)

These are the serious cases. Section 30 provides imprisonment that may extend to life and a fine up to ten lakh rupees. For a first offence the minimum is five years and a fine of one lakh rupees; for a second or later offence it is ten years' rigorous imprisonment and a fine of five lakh rupees. Section 32 places the burden on the accused to account for the liquor found in his possession, vehicle or premises. Offences under the Act are cognizable and non-bailable (Section 76(1)), and, except Section 37 cases, are tried by Special Courts (Section 83, as substituted in 2022).

Is anticipatory bail available?

Section 76(2) of the Act says that the provision on anticipatory bail (Section 438 of the old Code of Criminal Procedure) does not apply to arrests under the Act. In Ram Vinay Yadav v. State of Bihar, Criminal Appeal (SJ) No. 431 of 2019, decided on 17 May 2019 (report), a Full Bench of the Patna High Court held that anticipatory bail petitions in prohibition cases are not maintainable, except where the facts show, prima facie, that no ingredient of the offence is made out. Anticipatory bail is now governed by Section 482 of the Bharatiya Nagarik Suraksha Sanhita, 2023 (BNSS); how the bar applies to it should be checked against current Patna High Court decisions before filing.

Regular bail: Special Court first, then the High Court

After arrest, the bail application is first filed before the Special Court (Excise). If it is rejected, a bail petition can be filed before the Patna High Court under Section 483 BNSS. Points usually examined include:

  • the quantity of liquor and where exactly it was recovered;
  • whether it was in the accused's conscious possession, or in a shared house or vehicle;
  • whether the accused owns the vehicle or premises named in the seizure list;
  • criminal antecedents under the Act;
  • the period already spent in custody, and whether the chargesheet has been filed.

Seized vehicle or house

A vehicle, animal or premises used in an offence is liable to confiscation (Section 56). The Collector must give a hearing before ordering confiscation (Section 58). After the 2022 amendment, the Collector can release the vehicle or premises on payment of a penalty notified by the State Government (Section 57B). An illegal confiscation order can be challenged under the Act and, where needed, before the Patna High Court.

Documents needed for bail

  • Copy of the FIR and the seizure list
  • Order of the Special Court rejecting bail, if any
  • Custody certificate from the jail
  • Chargesheet, if filed
  • Details of any earlier case against the accused
  • Ownership papers of the vehicle or premises (RC, deed, rent receipts)

Frequently asked questions

Is a first-time drinking case bailable in Bihar?

Yes. A first offence under Section 37(1) is bailable under Section 76(1). After the 2022 amendment the person is produced before an Executive Magistrate and released on paying the penalty notified by the State Government; non-payment invites one month's simple imprisonment.

Can I get anticipatory bail in a liquor case in Bihar?

Section 76(2) bars anticipatory bail under the Act. A Full Bench of the Patna High Court (Ram Vinay Yadav, 2019) held such petitions not maintainable unless, prima facie, no ingredient of the offence is made out. Check the current position under BNSS Section 482 before filing.

Where do I apply for bail in a possession case?

First before the Special Court (Excise). If bail is rejected, a bail petition can be filed in the Patna High Court under Section 483 BNSS.

What is the punishment for possession or sale of liquor?

Under Section 30, imprisonment up to life and fine up to ten lakh rupees. A first offence carries at least five years and one lakh rupees fine; a repeat offence at least ten years and five lakh rupees.

Liquor was found in my car but it was not mine. What helps?

Section 32 puts the burden on the person in possession to explain. Who was driving, who owns the car, where the liquor was found and the seizure list all matter, and should be placed before the court with documents.

How can a seized vehicle be released?

The Collector may release it on payment of the penalty notified by the State Government (Section 57B). Otherwise it can be confiscated after a hearing under Section 58.

This article is general information about the Bihar Prohibition and Excise Act and is not legal advice. The Act, its rules and notified penalties are amended from time to time; check the current position or consult an advocate about your own matter.
