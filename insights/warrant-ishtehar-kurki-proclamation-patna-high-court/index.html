Warrant, Ishtehar or Kurki Issued Against You in Bihar? What It Means and What to Do

By Pankaj Kumar Choudhary, Advocate, Patna High Court · Choudhary Law Chambers, Patna · 25 November 2026

In Bihar, families often panic when the police come with a "warrant", paste an "ishtehar" on the house, or threaten "kurki" (attachment of property). These are three steps that a court can order when an accused does not appear. This guide explains them in simple words, with two 2025 orders of the Patna High Court.

In this article

  2. What the Patna High Court said

First, learn these words

  • Warrant (NBW): a court order telling the police to arrest a person and bring him to court. A non-bailable warrant (NBW) means the police do not release him on the spot.
  • Ishtehar (proclamation): a public notice by the court, under Section 84 BNSS (old Section 82 CrPC), asking the person to appear by a fixed date.
  • Kurki (attachment): an order to attach the person's property, under Section 85 BNSS (old Section 83 CrPC), if he still does not appear.
  • Proclaimed offender: a person declared absconding after an ishtehar in serious cases.
  • Anticipatory bail: protection from arrest granted before arrest.
  • Recall: asking the same court to take back (cancel) its warrant.

What the Patna High Court said

  • Police must really try before asking for a warrant: In Karnvir Singh Yadav v. State of Bihar, Patna High Court, Cr. Misc. No. 22151 of 2025, decided on 23 April 2025, a murder case from Barh, the court found that the "police did not make any serious efforts to nab the petitioner/accused before pressing the prayer of NBW". It said the burden of arrest and investigation is on the police and "cannot be diluted merely by fulfilling the legal formalities". It directed the DGP, Bihar to enquire personally and asked that the Supreme Court's guidelines on warrants be circulated to district judges. On the facts, it did not quash the warrant and proclamation.
  • Anticipatory bail is possible even after ishtehar or kurki: In Mangali Devi v. State of Bihar, Patna High Court, Cr. Misc. No. 33213 of 2025, decided on 27 August 2025, the court held that an anticipatory bail petition "is maintainable, even if the proceeding under Sections 82 and 83" has started. Whether bail is granted depends on the facts. In that case of cross-FIRs from a fight, bail was granted on surrender or arrest within eight weeks, with bonds of Rs. 10,000 and two sureties.

What to do, step by step

  1. Find out exactly what was issued: warrant, ishtehar or kurki, the case number and the court. Check the case and orders on eCourts: https://services.ecourts.gov.in/ecourtindia_v6/
  2. Get certified copies of the warrant / ishtehar / kurki orders and the FIR.
  3. Do not hide from the court. Absconding makes bail harder; courts look at whether the person tried to cooperate.
  4. Apply in the trial court to recall the warrant and to appear, or apply for anticipatory bail in the Sessions Court, depending on the stage.
  5. If refused or the order is illegal, a petition can be filed in the Patna High Court. Case status: https://patnahighcourt.gov.in/

An imaginary example

Suresh (an imaginary name) works in Gujarat. His family in Saran tells him that an ishtehar has been pasted on their house in an old land-fight case. He never received any summons. His advocate first checks the case on eCourts and gets copies of the orders. Since an ishtehar has been issued, the advocate explains that anticipatory bail can still be sought, as the Patna High Court held in Mangali Devi, but the court will look at all the facts, including why Suresh did not appear earlier.

Every case depends on its own facts. No result can be promised.

Frequently asked questions

What is the difference between warrant, ishtehar and kurki?

A warrant orders arrest; an ishtehar (Section 84 BNSS) publicly asks the person to appear; kurki (Section 85 BNSS) attaches property if he still does not appear.

Can I get anticipatory bail after an ishtehar or kurki?

The petition is maintainable, as the Patna High Court held in Mangali Devi (2025), but grant depends on the facts of the case.

Can police get a warrant without trying to find me?

In Karnvir Singh Yadav (2025) the Patna High Court criticised the police for seeking an NBW without serious efforts to arrest, and directed the DGP to enquire.

How do I check if a warrant has been issued?

Check the case and orders on eCourts using the case number or CNR, and get certified copies from the court.
