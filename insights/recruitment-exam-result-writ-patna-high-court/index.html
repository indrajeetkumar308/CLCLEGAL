Recruitment Exam Result or Selection Problem? What the Patna High Court Says, Explained Simply

By Pankaj Kumar Choudhary, Advocate, Patna High Court · Choudhary Law Chambers, Patna · 19 November 2026

Lakhs of young people in Bihar appear in BPSC, BSSC, police and teacher recruitment exams. When an answer key looks wrong, a result is stopped, or a name is dropped at document verification, many think of going to the High Court. The Patna High Court can examine such cases under Article 226 of the Constitution. But it has also made clear what it will and will not do. This guide explains it simply.

  2. What the Patna High Court has said

First, learn these words

  • Answer key / model answer: the correct answers published by the commission.
  • Candidature: your right to take part in the selection.
  • Document verification: checking your certificates before selection.
  • Interim relief: a temporary order while the case is pending.
  • Necessary party: a person who will be affected by the court's order and must be made a party.

What the Patna High Court has said

  • Answer key disputes are hard to win. In Vivek Pandey v. State of Bihar, CWJC No. 11556 of 2021, decided on 11 November 2022 (BPSC Assistant Prosecution Officer exam), the court relied on the Supreme Court's principles: "The court should presume the correctness of the key answers" and "In the event of a doubt, the benefit should go to the examination authority rather than to the candidate." It also said "public interest demands that the selection process is not disturbed." The writ petitions were dismissed.
  • Wrong information in the form can cost the job. In Union of India v. Shreya, CWJC No. 18157 of 2025, decided on 3 April 2026, the online form showed Hindi in Class 10, but the mark sheet did not. The Division Bench upheld the termination, saying the authorities "are bound to correct the mistake and recall the order of selection".
  • Interim stay is not automatic. In CWJC No. 369 of 2025, order dated 16 January 2025 (BPSC 70th preliminary exam), the court said: "I am not inclined to grant any interim relief", while keeping the result subject to the final outcome of the case.

When a writ is more likely to help

From these cases, a writ has a better chance where the commission breaks its own advertisement or rules, treats similar candidates differently, or rejects a candidate without a clear reason, and the candidate has filled every detail truthfully. Courts are slow to interfere with expert answer keys.

What to do, step by step

  1. Save everything: advertisement, online form, admit card, answer key, objections, result, and every notice.
  2. File objections in time before the commission, if it allows them, and keep proof.
  3. Check your own form: every detail must match your certificates.
  4. Act quickly: delay can hurt in selection matters. Candidates who will be affected may need to be made parties.
  5. File the writ in the Patna High Court with all papers, and track it on the Case Status page: https://patnahighcourt.gov.in/

An imaginary example

Amit (an imaginary name) from Siwan is not called for the interview. The commission says his caste certificate was late, but he has the receiving showing he uploaded it before the last date. His advocate files a writ in the Patna High Court with the advertisement, the receiving and the rejection letter. The court can examine whether the commission followed its own advertisement.

Every case depends on its own facts. No result can be promised.

Frequently asked questions

Can the High Court change a wrong answer key?

Rarely. In Vivek Pandey (2022) the Patna High Court applied the rule that courts presume key answers are correct and give the benefit of doubt to the examination authority.

Will the High Court stop the result at once?

Not automatically. In CWJC 369 of 2025 (BPSC 70th) the court refused interim relief but kept the result subject to the final decision.

What if my form had a wrong detail?

It can be serious. In Union of India v. Shreya (2026) the Division Bench upheld termination where the form did not match the mark sheet.

Where is the writ filed?

In the Patna High Court under Article 226 of the Constitution, for recruitments in Bihar.
