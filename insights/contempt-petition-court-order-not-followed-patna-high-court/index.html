Court Order Not Followed? A Simple Guide to a Contempt Petition in the Patna High Court

By Pankaj Kumar Choudhary, Advocate, Patna High Court · Choudhary Law Chambers, Patna · 18 November 2026

Many people win a writ petition in the Patna High Court. The court tells a government officer to pay salary, decide an application or restore something. Then months pass and nothing happens. The law gives a way to make the order count: a contempt petition. This guide explains it in simple words.

In this article
  1. First, learn these words
  2. What the law says
  3. Real examples from the Patna High Court
  4. What to do, step by step

First, learn these words

  • Contempt of court: not respecting or not obeying a court.
  • Civil contempt: wilfully not obeying a court's judgment, order or direction.
  • Wilful: done knowingly and on purpose, not by honest mistake.
  • Contempt petition: an application asking the court to act against the person who is not obeying its order.
  • Compliance: doing what the order says.

What the law says

The Contempt of Courts Act, 1971 deals with this. In Maheshwar Peri v. High Court of Judicature at Allahabad, Supreme Court, 30 June 2016, the Supreme Court referred to civil contempt as "the wilful disobedience of any judgment, decree, order etc." It also quoted Section 20 of the Act: no court shall initiate contempt proceedings "after the expiry of a period of one year from the date on which the contempt is alleged to have been committed." So do not wait too long.

Real examples from the Patna High Court

  • Read the order carefully: In Deodhira Sanskrit Uchcha Vidyalaya v. State of Bihar, LPA No. 1417 of 2019, decided on 11 December 2023, the writ order had only directed the Circle Officer to consider the claim and pass a reasoned order. The Division Bench found "absolutely no violation" and dismissed the appeal. If the order only says "consider", a reasoned decision, even against you, may count as compliance.
  • An appeal alone does not stop contempt: In Anil Kumar v. State of Bihar, M.J.C. No. 3368 of 2024, order dated 12 September 2025, the officers said an appeal was pending. The court said that "if the opposite party fails to bring a stay order from the Division Bench, the instant contempt proceeding shall continue."

What to do, step by step

  1. Get the certified copy of the High Court order.
  2. Read exactly what it directs: who must do what, and by when.
  3. Send the order with a written request to the officer, and keep the receiving.
  4. If nothing happens: file a contempt petition in the Patna High Court, well within one year, with the order, your request and its receiving.
  5. Track the case on the Case Status page of the Patna High Court: https://patnahighcourt.gov.in/

An imaginary example

Sushila (an imaginary name), a retired teacher from Nalanda, wins a writ. The High Court directs the department to pay her pending pension within three months. Five months pass and nothing is paid. She sends the order with a written request to the officer and keeps the receiving. Still nothing. Her advocate files a contempt petition in the Patna High Court, well within one year. The court can call the officer to explain why its order was not followed.

Every case depends on its own facts. No result can be promised.

Frequently asked questions

What is civil contempt?

Wilful disobedience of a court judgment, decree, order or direction. The Supreme Court referred to it in these words in Maheshwar Peri (2016).

Is there a time limit?

Yes. As quoted by the Supreme Court in Maheshwar Peri (2016), Section 20 of the Contempt of Courts Act, 1971 bars initiating contempt after one year from the date the contempt is alleged to have been committed.

The officer has filed an appeal. Does contempt stop?

Not by itself. In Anil Kumar (2025) the Patna High Court said the contempt would continue unless a stay order was brought from the Division Bench.

The order only said "consider". Is a rejection contempt?

Usually not. In Deodhira Sanskrit Uchcha Vidyalaya (2023) the Patna High Court found no violation where the order had directed only consideration of the claim.
