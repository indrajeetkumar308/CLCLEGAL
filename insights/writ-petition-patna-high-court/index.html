Writ Petition in Patna High Court: Article 226 Explained

By Pankaj Kumar Choudhary, Advocate, Patna High Court · Choudhary Law Chambers, Patna · 6 November 2026

Quick answer: A writ petition asks a High Court to review certain unlawful acts, failures or decisions, commonly involving public authorities. Article 226 of the Constitution of India empowers the Patna High Court, within its territorial jurisdiction, to issue appropriate writs, directions and orders. A writ is not an automatic substitute for an appeal or a guarantee that the court will change an administrative decision.

1. A simple example: a government office refuses to act

Imagine that Sita applies to a Bihar government office for a certificate. She supplies the required documents, but the office gives no decision and no explanation for a long time. She wants the office to act lawfully.

Her first step might be to check the department's complaint, appeal or other prescribed procedure. In an appropriate case, if a public authority has failed to perform a legally required duty, she may seek a direction from the High Court. That direction is sometimes called a writ of mandamus.

This example is imaginary. The correct remedy depends on the particular law, official order, deadlines and facts.

2. What is a writ petition under Article 226?

A writ petition is a request for judicial relief against an unlawful exercise of public power, failure to perform a public duty or another matter falling within constitutional writ jurisdiction. Article 226 allows High Courts to act for enforcement of fundamental rights and for other legally recognized purposes. The power is discretionary: the High Court decides whether this extraordinary route is appropriate.

Read the Constitution of India, Part VI (official Government of India PDF), particularly Articles 226 and 227. Article 227 concerns the High Court's separate supervisory jurisdiction over courts and tribunals; it is not simply another name for Article 226.

3. What are the five main types of writs?

  • Habeas Corpus — produce a detained person: Used to question allegedly unlawful detention and have the detained person brought before the court.
  • Mandamus — perform a public legal duty: May require an authority to carry out a duty the law places on it. It is not a tool for making an official decide in someone's favour when lawful discretion remains.
  • Certiorari — examine and set aside an unlawful decision: May be used against qualifying judicial or quasi-judicial decisions where a recognized legal defect exists.
  • Prohibition — stop proceedings beyond jurisdiction: May restrain an inferior court or tribunal from proceeding without lawful authority.
  • Quo Warranto — question entitlement to public office: Used to examine the legal authority by which a person holds a qualifying public office.

The label matters less than the facts, legal duty, jurisdiction and relief requested. Not every problem fits neatly into one of these labels; Article 226 also permits appropriate directions and orders.

4. When may someone consider a writ petition in Bihar?

Depending on the governing statute and available remedies, common questions can involve:

  • Government department inaction: An application or representation is not being dealt with despite an enforceable legal duty.
  • Revenue and land authorities: An order is allegedly passed without jurisdiction or without giving a hearing. Revenue appeal or revision procedures may have to be used first.
  • Public employment: A government or public-authority action is challenged for a legally recognized reason, subject to the proper service-law forum and procedure.
  • Licences, tenders and permissions: Public-law illegality is alleged in an authority's decision. The court does not normally re-score business judgments merely because a party is unhappy.
  • Detention or serious rights issues: A constitutional remedy may be relevant when facts meet the legal requirements.

Important: An ordinary ownership dispute between two private individuals usually calls for an appropriate civil or statutory remedy rather than assuming a writ petition will settle title.

5. Real-life-style examples: when a writ may help—and when it may not

The following are fictional teaching examples, not actual client matters or predictions about a court decision. Each has a different possible legal route. Even similar-looking cases can require different proceedings because the statute and supporting documents differ.

Example A: A government office does not decide an application (mandamus)

Situation: Meena applies to a statutory authority in Bihar for a licence. The rules require the authority to consider the application. Months pass without an order, even after she follows the prescribed reminder and grievance steps.

Possible question: Could the High Court direct the authority to consider and decide her application in accordance with law? In an appropriate case, a direction resembling mandamus may be considered. The court would not necessarily order the authority to grant the licence.

First check: Is there a time limit or a special complaint/appeal mechanism under the licensing statute? Keep the acknowledgement, reminder and applicable rule.

Example B: A land-mutation application is rejected (appeal may come first)

Situation: After buying land, Raj receives a rejection order from the Circle Officer. He believes the order overlooks his registered sale deed.

Possible route: A statutory DCLR mutation appeal may be the ordinary first step. A writ is not automatically the next step just because Raj disagrees with the Circle Officer. If a competent authority acted without jurisdiction or seriously denied a hearing, the constitutional question may require separate examination.

First check: Obtain the signed order, note its date and examine the applicable appeal deadline. If ownership is genuinely contested, a civil suit may be necessary.

Example C: A public recruitment decision is made without a fair process

Situation: Priya applies for a Bihar public-authority post. A decision affecting her candidature is taken using a condition that she believes contradicts the published recruitment rules.

Possible question: Is there a review or service tribunal route? Does the decision involve a legally enforceable rule or unequal treatment? A writ may be relevant in an appropriate case, but the court will not simply appoint Priya because she considers the process unfair.

First check: Preserve the recruitment notice, eligibility provisions, results, communications and representation. Identify the proper statutory forum.

Example D: A public authority cancels a business permission without a hearing

Situation: A small business receives an order cancelling its permission. The governing rules appear to require prior notice, but the business says it received none.

Possible question: Was there a breach of natural justice, such as failure to provide a hearing when legally required? A writ may sometimes be considered despite a statutory appeal, but the facts and the governing law matter. Some statutes validly permit urgent action followed by a hearing.

First check: Collect the cancellation order, service records, relevant statutory rule, proof of compliance and details of any available appeal. Filing alone does not stay the cancellation.

Example E: Someone is allegedly held without lawful authority (habeas corpus)

Situation: A family cannot contact an adult relative and has credible information suggesting unlawful custody by officials.

Possible route: An appropriate habeas corpus petition can seek judicial examination of the detention. This is a serious, fact-sensitive remedy; where detention is under a valid judicial order, different legal remedies may be required.

First check: Record the last known location, available evidence and the authority alleged to be holding the person. Urgent safety concerns should also be taken to appropriate emergency authorities.

Example F: A neighbour has not repaid a private loan (usually not a writ)

Situation: Arjun lent money to a friend. The friend refuses to repay it, and Arjun wants the High Court to issue a writ ordering repayment.

Why a writ may be unsuitable: This is ordinarily a private recovery dispute, not failure by a public body to perform a public duty. A civil or other appropriate statutory remedy should be examined instead.

Key lesson: Before asking “Can I file a writ?”, identify who has the legal duty, what law creates that duty, what happened, and what other remedy is available.

6. Can a writ be filed against a private person or company?

Article 226 is not limited to government bodies by its wording, but a writ against a private body generally requires an identifiable public duty or public-law element. A purely private payment disagreement or ordinary commercial contract breach normally belongs in the forum provided by contract or law. The nature of the duty, not merely the organisation's name, is significant.

7. Must an appeal be filed before approaching the High Court?

Often, the available statutory appeal or revision should be considered first. The Patna High Court may decline a writ when an effective alternative remedy exists. However, the Supreme Court has explained recognized exceptions, including fundamental-rights enforcement, violations of natural justice, action wholly without jurisdiction and challenges to the constitutional validity of legislation.

In Radha Krishan Industries v. State of Himachal Pradesh (2021), the Supreme Court explained these principles and stressed that the alternative-remedy rule is generally one of judicial discretion, not an absolute bar in every situation. Read the official Supreme Court judgment (20 April 2021).

8. Can a writ challenge a Bihar land-mutation or DCLR order?

Potentially, but not every adverse mutation order is a writ case. Bihar land-mutation law may provide an appeal to the DCLR and further revision through the revenue hierarchy. A dispute over actual ownership may need a civil suit instead. A writ court considers jurisdiction, legality, procedural fairness and the availability of other remedies; it does not ordinarily conduct a full ownership trial on contested evidence.

For the ordinary statutory process, read the Bihar mutation appeal guide and the Bihar revenue forums guide.

9. How to file a writ petition in the Patna High Court

  1. Identify the public-law issue: Note the specific order, omission, legal duty, authority and date.
  2. Check the right forum and alternative remedy: Read the statute and any applicable appeal/revision provision; identify limitation periods.
  3. Collect the record: Obtain the impugned order or rejection, representations, acknowledgements, relevant notices and supporting evidence.
  4. Prepare the petition: Set out material facts chronologically, legal grounds, the parties, jurisdiction, relief sought and any interim relief requested. Required affidavits and annexures depend on the applicable rules.
  5. Follow current court filing rules: Use the Patna High Court's official website to check filing, e-filing, scrutiny, case-number allocation and other procedural requirements.
  6. Track the proceeding: After registration, monitor case status, cause lists and orders. The court may issue notice, seek a response, grant or decline interim relief, or dispose of the case according to law.

For official procedural information, see the Patna High Court website. Filing routes, court fees, formatting and registry requirements should be checked against the rules applicable on the filing date. This overview is not a ready-to-file pleading.

10. What documents are commonly needed?

  • The government order, notice or communication being challenged, if any.
  • Copies of applications, representations and proof they were submitted.
  • Relevant statutory rules, permits, service records or revenue records.
  • Documents showing dates, the authority involved and how the petitioner is affected.
  • Copies of appeal/revision proceedings and orders, where applicable.
  • A clear chronology and documents supporting the relief sought.

The list is illustrative. Some cases, such as detention matters, require different evidence and urgent procedures.

11. Will filing a writ petition stop the government order?

No automatic stay follows from filing. Interim protection or stay depends on a specific judicial order. The court may refuse interim relief even while hearing the main petition. Never assume that a deadline, recovery action, licence cancellation or other order has been suspended merely because a case number was issued.

12. How much time does a writ petition take?

There is no universal time limit for deciding every writ petition. Urgency, the record, notices, disputed facts, court listing and the relief requested affect the timeline. A fixed duration or favourable result cannot responsibly be promised. Delaying action can also affect the court's assessment; prompt review of dates is important.

13. Article 226 versus Article 32 — what is the difference?

Article 226: A High Court may enforce fundamental rights and grant relief for other legally recognized purposes within its constitutional jurisdiction. Article 32: The Supreme Court's constitutional remedy focuses on enforcement of fundamental rights. Choosing the right forum depends on the claim, geography, statutory framework and available remedies.

14. Common mistakes to avoid

  • Assuming every wrong government decision must be challenged by writ instead of the statutory appeal.
  • Asking for a favourable outcome without identifying an enforceable legal right or duty.
  • Omitting the exact order, date, prior proceedings or relevant facts.
  • Treating Article 226 and Article 227 as identical.
  • Assuming a writ automatically stops execution of the challenged action.

Frequently asked questions

Can I file a writ petition in the Patna High Court against a Bihar government department?

In an appropriate public-law dispute within the court's jurisdiction, yes. The law, duty involved, alternative remedies and supporting facts determine whether a writ is suitable.

Can I file a writ if a government office does not reply to my application?

Sometimes. It depends on whether the authority has a legal duty to act, whether a prescribed complaint or appeal route exists, and the surrounding facts.

Is a writ petition the same as an appeal?

No. A statutory appeal follows the review process established by a particular law. Writ jurisdiction is an extraordinary constitutional remedy.

Does filing an Article 226 writ automatically stay an order?

No. A specific interim order is needed for any stay or protective direction.

Can a writ petition decide ownership of disputed land?

Ordinary contested title questions commonly require a civil or appropriate statutory proceeding. A writ is generally not a substitute for a full evidence-based trial.

What are the five writs under Article 226?

Habeas corpus, mandamus, certiorari, prohibition and quo warranto.
