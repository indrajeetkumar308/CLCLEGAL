Mutual Consent vs Contested Divorce in India: Legal Process and Differences

Legal information · Family and matrimonial law · 31 October 2026

Indian divorce procedure depends on the law governing a marriage, the grounds available under that law and whether both spouses consent to the dissolution. This educational overview primarily compares Section 13B (mutual consent) and Section 13 (contested divorce) of the Hindu Marriage Act, 1955. The Special Marriage Act, 1954 contains distinct provisions.

1. What is mutual consent divorce?

Under Section 13B(1) of the Hindu Marriage Act, both spouses may jointly petition for dissolution when they have been living separately for one year or more, have been unable to live together and mutually agree that the marriage should be dissolved. “Living separately” is evaluated from the circumstances and does not invariably require different residential addresses. A court must examine whether the statutory requirements and consent are genuine.

2. What is contested divorce?

In a contested divorce, one spouse seeks a decree on a legally recognised ground even if the other spouse does not agree. Section 13 of the Hindu Marriage Act includes grounds such as cruelty, adultery and desertion, each subject to its statutory conditions. Desertion under the relevant provision generally requires a continuous period of at least two years immediately before the petition. Allegations must be established through the applicable procedure and evidence; ordinary disagreement alone does not automatically establish a ground.

4. Usual mutual consent procedure

  1. Identify the law: Determine whether the marriage falls under the Hindu Marriage Act, Special Marriage Act or another legal framework.
  2. Consider connected questions: Parties may address maintenance, custody, education, access arrangements and relevant financial issues, where applicable.
  3. File a joint petition: State the material facts and satisfaction of statutory conditions before the competent court.
  4. First motion: The court examines the petition and statements as appropriate.
  5. Second motion and decree: The court considers the continued consent and legal requirements. The marriage ends when the competent court grants a decree, not merely when a private settlement is signed.

5. Is the six-month interval always mandatory?

Section 13B(2) provides for a second motion after the prescribed interval. In Amardeep Singh v. Harveen Kaur (2017), the Supreme Court held that the six-month cooling-off period can be waived in an appropriate case. Waiver is discretionary, not automatic; the court evaluates circumstances including the prospects of reconciliation and settlement of outstanding issues. The one-year separation requirement in Section 13B(1) is a distinct statutory condition.

6. How does a contested case proceed?

The broad stages can involve filing a petition, service of notice, the respondent's reply, any court-directed reconciliation efforts, determination of issues, evidence and hearing, followed by judgment. The precise procedure and duration depend on the law, pleadings, facts and judicial directions. A particular outcome or completion time cannot be assured.

7. Maintenance and children's welfare

Under the Hindu Marriage Act, Section 24 concerns maintenance during proceedings and litigation expenses; Section 25 concerns permanent alimony; and Section 26 addresses custody, maintenance and education of minor children. These questions are evaluated separately under the applicable facts and law. The welfare of children is a central consideration in custody decisions.

8. Do all marriages follow the same law?

No. The Special Marriage Act, 1954 provides for contested divorce under Section 27 and mutual consent under Section 28. Other marriages may be governed by different statutes or personal laws. This article does not apply every Hindu Marriage Act condition to every marriage in India.

Frequently asked questions

Can one spouse obtain mutual consent divorce without the other's agreement?

No. A mutual consent decree requires valid consent and compliance with the applicable legal conditions.

Can divorce proceedings ordinarily begin within the first year of marriage?

Section 14 of the Hindu Marriage Act ordinarily restricts presentation of a divorce petition in the first year of marriage, subject to a statutory leave exception for exceptional hardship or exceptional depravity.

Does signing a settlement agreement itself dissolve the marriage?

No. Where the governing law requires a court decree, the marriage is not dissolved merely by signing an agreement.

Can the court waive the six-month interval in every case?

No. Waiver under the applicable judicial principles depends on the facts and the court's discretion.
