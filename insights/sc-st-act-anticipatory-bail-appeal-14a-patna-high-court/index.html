SC/ST Act Case and Anticipatory Bail Refused? The Section 14A Appeal in the Patna High Court, Explained Simply

By Pankaj Kumar Choudhary, Advocate, Patna High Court · Choudhary Law Chambers, Patna · 19 November 2026

Cases under the Scheduled Castes and the Scheduled Tribes (Prevention of Atrocities) Act, 1989 are serious, and the Act protects people from real atrocities. At the same time, in many villages such cases arise out of land quarrels where both sides blame each other. When the Special Court refuses bail, the next step is not an ordinary bail petition but an appeal under Section 14A of the Act to the High Court. This guide explains it simply.

First, learn these words

  • SC/ST Act: the law that punishes atrocities against members of Scheduled Castes and Scheduled Tribes.
  • Special Court: the court that tries cases under this Act.
  • Section 14A appeal: an appeal to the High Court against certain orders of the Special Court, including orders on bail.
  • Anticipatory bail: bail ordered before arrest.
  • Counter-case: a case filed by the other side about the same quarrel.

Real examples from the Patna High Court

  • In Pankaj Kumar Sinha v. State of Bihar, Cr. Appeal (SJ) No. 3251 of 2025, decided on 10 November 2025, an appeal under Section 14A(2) against refusal of anticipatory bail was allowed, noting the "possibility of false accusation and dubious nature of the case", on a bail bond of Rs. 10,000 and the conditions of Section 482(2) BNSS.
  • In Manoj Mukhiya v. State of Bihar, Cr. Appeal (SJ) No. 4394 of 2024, decided on 20 November 2025, the court allowed the appeal "considering the doubtful nature of accusation and clean antecedent of the appellants", directing them to appear on every date.
  • In Saroja Devi v. State of Bihar, Cr. Appeal (SJ) No. 4920 of 2024, decided on 17 July 2026, the court allowed the appeal "considering the background of land dispute" and the possibility of false implication.

In each case the appellants were asked to surrender or be arrested within eight weeks and then be released on bail. Every case turns on its own facts; where the accusation is clear and serious, relief can be refused.

What to do, step by step

  1. Get the FIR and note the exact SC/ST Act sections.
  2. Apply for anticipatory bail in the Special Court and get a certified copy of the order.
  3. If refused, file an appeal under Section 14A in the Patna High Court, not an ordinary bail petition.
  4. Show the background: land dispute, counter-case, delay in FIR, your clean record.
  5. Track the case on the Patna High Court Case Status page: https://patnahighcourt.gov.in/

An imaginary example

Mohan (an imaginary name) from Gaya has a boundary dispute with his neighbour. Both sides file cases; the neighbour's FIR includes SC/ST Act sections. The Special Court refuses Mohan anticipatory bail. His advocate files an appeal under Section 14A in the Patna High Court, with both FIRs and the land papers, so that the court can see the full background.

Every case depends on its own facts. No result can be promised.

Frequently asked questions

Special Court refused bail in an SC/ST case. What next?

An appeal under Section 14A of the SC/ST (Prevention of Atrocities) Act lies to the High Court; in Bihar, the Patna High Court.

Does the High Court grant relief in such appeals?

In suitable cases. In 2025–2026 orders such as Pankaj Kumar Sinha, Manoj Mukhiya and Saroja Devi, appeals were allowed where the accusation looked doubtful or arose from a land dispute.

What conditions are usually imposed?

In these orders: surrender or arrest within eight weeks, bail bonds, the conditions of Section 482(2) BNSS, and appearance on every date.

Is every SC/ST case false?

No. The Act protects against real atrocities. Each case is decided on its own facts.
