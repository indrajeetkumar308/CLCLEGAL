Father or Mother Died in a Government Job? Compassionate Appointment (Anukampa Niyukti) in Bihar, Explained Simply

By Pankaj Kumar Choudhary, Advocate, Patna High Court · Choudhary Law Chambers, Patna · 24 November 2026

When a government employee dies while still in service, the family suddenly loses its income. To help such families, governments have schemes for compassionate appointment, called anukampa niyukti in Bihar. Every year many families in Bihar apply, and many claims are delayed or rejected. This guide explains, in simple words, how it works and what the courts have recently said.

  2. What the courts have said
  3. Common reasons for rejection

First, learn these words

  • Compassionate appointment (anukampa niyukti): a job given to a dependent of an employee who died in service, so the family can survive the sudden crisis.
  • Dependent: a family member who depended on the deceased employee, such as the spouse, son or daughter, as defined in the applicable rules.
  • Compassionate Committee: the government committee that examines these applications.
  • Gainfully employed: having a regular job or income.
  • Writ petition: a case in the High Court against an illegal or unfair decision of a government authority.
  • Abeyance: keeping a matter pending without deciding it.

What the courts have said

  • It is help, not a right: In Canara Bank v. Ajithkumar G.K., 2025 INSC 184, Supreme Court, decided on 11 February 2025, the Court said "Compassionate appointment is a concession and not a right". It also said that family pension or terminal benefits "cannot be treated as substitute" for employment assistance.
  • The claim must be decided, not kept pending: In Atul Chauhan v. State of Haryana, 2026 INSC 640, Supreme Court, decided on 11 June 2026, a son's claim was kept pending and then rejected. The Court held that a rule about financial assistance could not be used to hold back a claim for compassionate appointment, and directed the State to decide the claim on merits within three months.
  • If another dependent has a regular job: In Rajeev Kumar v. State of Bihar, Patna High Court, LPA No. 718 of 2025, decided on 22 September 2026, the claim was rejected because the applicant's elder brother worked in a bank. Following its Full Bench ruling, the High Court said that where any dependent "is gainfully employed, no other dependent would be entitled". The appeal was also dismissed for a delay of 771 days.

Common reasons for rejection

  • Another dependent of the family already has a regular job.
  • The application was filed late, or documents were missing.
  • The applicant did not meet the age or qualification in the rules.
  • The authority felt the family was no longer in financial crisis.

Not every rejection is correct. If the authority applied the wrong rule, ignored documents, did not give reasons, or simply sat on the file, the decision can be challenged.

What to do, step by step

  1. Apply quickly to the department where the employee worked, with the death certificate, service details, family details and educational certificates of the applicant.
  2. Keep proof of every application and reminder, with dates.
  3. If there is no decision for a long time, send a written representation asking for a decision.
  4. If the claim is rejected, get a copy of the order and read the reason carefully.
  5. File a writ petition in the Patna High Court without delay if the rejection or inaction is wrong. Do not wait for years: the Patna High Court dismissed one appeal partly because of delay. Case status: https://patnahighcourt.gov.in/

An imaginary example

Pooja (an imaginary name) from Ara lost her father, a clerk in a government office, in 2025. She applied for compassionate appointment the same year with all papers. Months passed with no reply. Her advocate sent a representation and, when there was still no decision, filed a writ petition asking the court to direct the department to decide her claim on merits. Whether she finally gets the job depends on the rules and her family's situation, but she is entitled to a proper decision.

Every case depends on its own facts. No result can be promised.

Frequently asked questions

Is compassionate appointment my right?

No. The Supreme Court said in Canara Bank (2025) that it is a concession and not a right. But the claim must be decided fairly under the rules.

My mother gets family pension. Can my claim be rejected for that reason alone?

In Canara Bank (2025) the Supreme Court said family pension or terminal benefits cannot be treated as a substitute for employment assistance.

My brother has a bank job. Can I still get compassionate appointment in Bihar?

In Rajeev Kumar (2026) the Patna High Court held that if any dependent is gainfully employed, no other dependent is entitled.

The department is not deciding my application. What can I do?

Send a written representation. If there is still no decision, a writ petition can be filed in the Patna High Court asking for a decision on merits.

Is there a time limit?

The rules usually fix time limits, and courts dislike long delays. In Rajeev Kumar the appeal was dismissed partly for a delay of 771 days, so act quickly.
