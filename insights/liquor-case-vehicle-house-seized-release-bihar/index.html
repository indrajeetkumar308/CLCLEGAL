Vehicle or House Seized in a Liquor Case in Bihar? How Release Works, Explained Simply

By Pankaj Kumar Choudhary, Advocate, Patna High Court · Choudhary Law Chambers, Patna · 19 November 2026

In Bihar, if liquor is found in a car, truck or house, the vehicle or the building can be seized under the Bihar Prohibition and Excise Act, 2016. Often the owner did not even know. Many families lose their vehicle or their rent income for months. The law gives an owner rights, and the Patna High Court has protected them in several cases. This guide explains it simply.

First, learn these words

  • Seizure: the police or excise officer taking the vehicle or sealing the property.
  • Confiscation: an order that the property now belongs to the State.
  • Collector: the district officer who decides confiscation.
  • Release on fine/penalty: getting the property back by paying an amount fixed under the rules.
  • Appeal: asking a senior officer to change the order.

What the law says (as quoted by the Patna High Court)

In Sharad Navnath Gange v. State of Bihar, CWJC No. 18561 of 2023, decided on 1 September 2025, the court quoted the Act:

  • Section 58(3): the Collector "shall, before passing an order under sub-section (2), give a reasonable opportunity to the person concerned, of being heard".
  • Section 92(1): final orders of an Excise Officer "shall be appealable to the Collector within sixty days from the date of the order."
  • Section 92(2): orders of the Collector go in appeal to the Excise Commissioner, and of the Excise Commissioner to the State Government, within ninety days.

Real examples from the Patna High Court

  • No extra 3% charge: In Bimlesh Kumar v. State of Bihar, CWJC No. 19659 of 2025, decided on 18 December 2025, a car was offered for release on 10% of insured value plus 3%. The court held that "the confiscating authority has no power to impose an additional 3% over and above the penalty amount", ordered a fresh order within 30 days, and said such extra amounts taken in other cases are liable to be refunded.
  • Landlord's house released: In Mahendra Prasad Singh v. State of Bihar, CWJC No. 2129 of 2025, decided on 1 September 2025, spirit was recovered from a rented shop. The court held the owner "could not be made to suffer for being the landlord/owner" where he had no knowledge, and directed release of the house.

What to do, step by step

  1. Get the papers: seizure list, FIR or excise case number, and any notice from the Collector.
  2. Prove ownership: RC, insurance, sale deed or rent agreement.
  3. Show you did not know: for example a rent agreement, a driver's details, or a theft complaint if the vehicle was stolen.
  4. Appear when called by the Collector and give a written reply.
  5. Appeal in time (60 or 90 days as above). If the order is against the law, a writ can be filed in the Patna High Court. Track it on https://patnahighcourt.gov.in/

An imaginary example

Rajesh (an imaginary name) from Vaishali gives his truck on hire with a driver. Police find liquor in the truck and seize it. Rajesh was at home and knew nothing. He collects the RC, the hire agreement and the driver's details, appears before the Collector, and files a written reply. If an order is passed against him without hearing him, his advocate can appeal in time or approach the Patna High Court.

Every case depends on its own facts. No result can be promised.

Frequently asked questions

Must the Collector hear me before confiscation?

Yes. Section 58(3), as quoted by the Patna High Court in Sharad Navnath Gange (2025), requires a reasonable opportunity of being heard.

What is the time limit for appeal?

As quoted in the same judgment: 60 days to the Collector against an Excise Officer's order, and 90 days for appeals against orders of the Collector or Excise Commissioner.

Can they charge an extra 3% for release?

No. In Bimlesh Kumar (2025) the Patna High Court held there is no power to impose an additional 3% over the penalty.

Liquor was found in my rented property. Will I lose it?

Not necessarily. In Mahendra Prasad Singh (2025) the court released a landlord's house where he had no knowledge.
