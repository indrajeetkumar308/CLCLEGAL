Case with Punishment up to 7 Years? The Police Notice under Section 35 BNSS, Explained Simply

By Pankaj Kumar Choudhary, Advocate, Patna High Court · Choudhary Law Chambers, Patna · 18 November 2026

Many people think that once an FIR is lodged, the police will come and arrest them at once. That is not always true. For many offences, the law asks the police to first decide whether arrest is really needed, and if it is not, to send a notice asking the person to come and join the investigation. This guide explains it in simple words.

First, learn these words

  • FIR: the first written report of a crime at the police station.
  • Arrest: the police taking a person into custody.
  • Notice of appearance: a written paper from the police asking you to come at a given time and place.
  • Investigating Officer (IO): the police officer looking into the case.
  • Recording reasons: the officer writing down why arrest is necessary.

What the law says

The rule is in Section 35 of the Bharatiya Nagarik Suraksha Sanhita, 2023 (BNSS). It took the place of Sections 41 and 41A of the old CrPC. A paper on this subject by a judicial officer, published on a district court website, explains:

  • for offences punishable with imprisonment up to seven years, "Police officers must record reasons showing necessity of arrest";
  • "Where arrest is not required, a notice to appear must be issued to the accused" under sub-section (3) of Section 35;
  • "If the person complies, he shall not be arrested, unless the IO records specific reasons for arrest."

These rules come from the Supreme Court judgment in Arnesh Kumar v. State of Bihar, (2014) 8 SCC 273, a case that started in Bihar. Another judicial training paper on a district court website notes that, as per the Supreme Court in Satender Kumar Antil, such notices "cannot be served via electronic modes such as WhatsApp, email, or SMS".

A real example from the Patna High Court

In Sita Sahu v. State of Bihar, Cr. WJC No. 1850 of 2025, order dated 14 August 2025, the petitioners said the offences were bailable and alleged non-compliance with Section 35 BNSS. The High Court ordered that "no coercive action shall be taken against the petitioners" and asked the State to file its reply.

What to do, step by step

  1. Get the FIR details: the sections, so you know the maximum punishment.
  2. If you get a notice: read the date, time and place. Keep the original safely.
  3. Go as the notice says and cooperate. Keep a copy or proof that you went.
  4. Fear arrest anyway? Speak to an advocate about anticipatory bail.
  5. If the rule is ignored: the matter can be raised before the court, and before the Patna High Court where needed.

An imaginary example

Pintu (an imaginary name) runs a small shop in Bhagalpur. After a quarrel, an FIR is lodged against him for an offence punishable up to 3 years. The police give him a written notice to come to the police station on a fixed date. He goes on that date with his papers and joins the investigation. Because he complied, the law protects him from arrest unless the officer records specific reasons.

Every case depends on its own facts. No result can be promised.

Frequently asked questions

Can the police arrest me at once in a case punishable up to 7 years?

For such offences the police must record reasons showing why arrest is necessary. Where arrest is not required, they must issue a notice to appear under Section 35(3) BNSS.

What if I follow the notice?

As explained in judicial training material, if the person complies, he shall not be arrested unless the officer records specific reasons for arrest.

Can the notice be sent on WhatsApp?

A judicial training paper notes the Supreme Court in Satender Kumar Antil said such notices cannot be served by WhatsApp, email or SMS.

Which old law does Section 35 replace?

Sections 41 and 41A of the old Code of Criminal Procedure.
