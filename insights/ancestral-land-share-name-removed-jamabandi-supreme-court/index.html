Brother or Uncle Removed Your Name from the Jamabandi? Your Share in Ancestral Land Stays, Says the Supreme Court

By Pankaj Kumar Choudhary, Advocate, Patna High Court · Choudhary Law Chambers, Patna · 23 November 2026

It happens in many Bihar families. Land of the father or grandfather is in the names of all the brothers. Years later, one brother finds that his name has quietly been removed from the jamabandi, and the land now stands in the name of his brother or nephew. Is his share gone? On 20 August 2026 the Supreme Court of India answered this question: no, a change in the revenue record alone does not take away anyone's share. (In Hindi, people search for this as "jamabandi se naam hata diya", "bhai ne zameen apne naam kara li" or "pushtaini zameen mein hissa".)

First, learn these words

  • Dakhil-kharij (mutation): changing the name in the government land record.
  • Jamabandi: the revenue register showing in whose name land rent (lagan) is collected.
  • Co-owner (sah-swami): each heir who shares ancestral or inherited land with others.
  • Relinquishment (hissa chhodna): when an heir gives up his share in favour of another.
  • Partition suit (batwara ka mukadma): a civil case asking the court to divide the land and give each owner a separate portion.
  • Limitation: the time limit within which a case must be filed.

The case in simple words

In Jamnabai and Others v. Vasudev and Others, 2026 INSC 900, Supreme Court, decided on 20 August 2026, about 12 acres of land near Indore came from a father to his two sons, Ramprasad and Vasudev, and was recorded in both names. Later, Ramprasad's name was removed from the revenue records and the land was mutated in the names of Vasudev and his son. Vasudev's side said that Ramprasad had given up his share, relying on an affidavit, a statement before the Naib Tehsildar and a consent letter. Ramprasad's heirs filed a suit for their share. The trial court and the first appeal court decided for the heirs, but the High Court reversed this in second appeal. The heirs went to the Supreme Court.

What the Supreme Court said

  • Mutation does not take away title: The Court said "an entry in the revenue record neither creates nor extinguishes title", and that the mutation could not "by itself extinguish Ramprasad's title".
  • Whoever says "he gave up his share" must prove it: "The burden of establishing such a relinquishment lay squarely on the respondents who asserted it". The heirs did not have to disprove it.
  • Time limit is counted from the real cause: The Court said "What matters is when the right to sue actually accrued", and that the possession of one co-owner is ordinarily treated as possession on behalf of all.
  • Result: The High Court's judgment was set aside and the trial court's decree was restored. The heirs were held to be co-owners, entitled to partition. Until lawful partition, the other side was restrained from selling the land or creating third-party rights.

What this means for Bihar families

  • If your name was removed from the jamabandi without a valid registered document, your share in family land does not end only because of that.
  • If someone claims that you or your father gave up the share, they must prove it properly.
  • Who gets how much share depends on the succession law that applies to the family; for Hindu families this is the Hindu Succession Act, 1956.
  • Do not delay. Time limits are set by the Limitation Act, 1963. Even though the time limit is counted from the real cause, acting early keeps evidence safe and stops the land from being sold to others.

What to do, step by step

  1. Check the jamabandi on the official Bihar e-Mutation portal and note when and on what basis your name was removed: https://emutation.bihar.gov.in/biharBhumireport/ViewJamabandi.aspx (main portal: https://biharbhumi.bihar.gov.in)
  2. Get certified copies of the mutation order and the papers it relied on from the Circle Office.
  3. Collect family papers: old khatiyan, the earlier jamabandi showing all names, a family tree (vanshavali) and any partition paper.
  4. Challenge the mutation under the Bihar Land Mutation Act, 2011 by appeal before the DCLR within the time allowed, if still open. See our guide linked below.
  5. File a partition suit in the civil court for your share, and ask for an order stopping sale of the land while the case runs. Case status can be tracked on eCourts: https://services.ecourts.gov.in/ecourtindia_v6/

An imaginary example

Mahesh (an imaginary name) lives in Delhi for work. His father and uncle had inherited 4 bigha of land in Bihta. In 2026 he finds that the jamabandi now shows only his uncle's son. His cousin says, "Your father gave up his share long ago." There is no registered document. Mahesh's advocate files a partition suit and asks for an injunction against sale. On the law laid down in Jamnabai, the cousin must prove the claimed relinquishment; the name change in the jamabandi alone does not end Mahesh's share.

Every case depends on its own facts. No result can be promised.

Frequently asked questions

My brother got the land mutated only in his name. Have I lost my share?

Not because of the mutation alone. In Jamnabai v. Vasudev (2026) the Supreme Court held that a revenue entry neither creates nor ends title.

He says my father gave up his share. Who has to prove it?

The person who claims the relinquishment. The Supreme Court said the burden lies on the side that asserts it.

Which court will give me my share?

The civil court, through a partition suit. The revenue authorities only keep the record.

After my father died, my brother got all the land mutated in his name. What can I do?

Collect the old jamabandi and khatiyan, challenge the mutation before the DCLR if time remains, and file a partition suit in the civil court for your share.

My father's name was removed. Do his children still get his share?

In Jamnabai the heirs of the brother whose name was removed were held to be co-owners and entitled to partition. Each case depends on its facts and the succession law that applies.

Can they sell the land while my case is going on?

You can ask the civil court for an injunction. In Jamnabai the Supreme Court restrained sale until lawful partition.
