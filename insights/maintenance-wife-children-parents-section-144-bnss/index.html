Maintenance for Wife, Children and Parents: A Simple Guide (Section 144 BNSS, Old Section 125 CrPC)

By Pankaj Kumar Choudhary, Advocate, Patna High Court · Choudhary Law Chambers, Patna · 18 November 2026

When a husband leaves his wife and children without money, or a grown-up son stops looking after his old parents, the law gives a quick remedy. The court can order a monthly amount, called maintenance. This guide explains it in simple words.

In this article
  1. First, learn these words
  2. Which law applies
  3. A recent Supreme Court ruling
  4. What to do, step by step
  5. An imaginary example
  6. Frequently asked questions

First, learn these words

  • Maintenance: a monthly amount for food, clothing and shelter, ordered by the court.
  • Neglect: not giving support to someone you are bound to support.
  • Sufficient means: enough income or ability to earn.
  • Applicant: the person asking for maintenance.
  • Family Court: the court that hears family and marriage cases.

Which law applies

Earlier it was Section 125 of the old CrPC. Now it is Section 144 of the Bharatiya Nagarik Suraksha Sanhita, 2023, as shown in the CrPC–BNSS comparison table of the Government of Tripura. The Supreme Court in Rajnesh v. Neha (copy on the Patna High Court website) quoted Section 125(1). It covers, among others:

  • "his wife, unable to maintain herself";
  • his minor child, whether legitimate or not;
  • "his father or mother, unable to maintain himself or herself".

The Court explained two conditions: the person has sufficient means, and he neglects or refuses to maintain them. It said the purpose is "to provide immediate relief to an applicant" and to prevent "vagrancy and destitution". Under Section 125(2), as quoted there, the allowance is payable "from the date of the order, or, if so ordered, from the date of the application".

A recent Supreme Court ruling

In Rina Kumari v. Dinesh Kumar Mahto, Supreme Court, 10 January 2025 (2025 INSC 55), the husband had got a decree for restitution of conjugal rights, but the wife did not return. The Court held that this decree alone "would not, by itself, be sufficient" to stop maintenance, and restored the Family Court's order of Rs. 10,000 per month.

What to do, step by step

  1. Collect proof: marriage or relationship, children's birth certificates, and the other person's job, income or property.
  2. Write down your own needs: rent, food, school fees, medicines.
  3. File the application in the Family Court of the district.
  4. Ask that maintenance be paid from the date of the application, if the court so orders.
  5. Track the case on the eCourts portal: https://services.ecourts.gov.in/ecourtindia_v6/

If either side is not satisfied with the order, it can be taken to the higher court, including the Patna High Court where the law allows. For divorce, read about mutual consent and contested divorce.

An imaginary example

Kavita (an imaginary name) lives in Muzaffarpur with her two small children. Her husband works in a private company but has stopped sending money. She collects her marriage photos, the children's birth certificates and proof of his job, and files an application in the Family Court. The court can order a monthly amount for her and the children.

Every case depends on its own facts. No result can be promised.

Frequently asked questions

Who can claim maintenance?

As quoted by the Supreme Court in Rajnesh v. Neha: a wife unable to maintain herself, minor children, and a father or mother unable to maintain himself or herself, among others.

Which section applies now?

Section 144 of the Bharatiya Nagarik Suraksha Sanhita, 2023. It replaced Section 125 of the old CrPC.

From which date is maintenance paid?

Under Section 125(2) as quoted by the Supreme Court: from the date of the order, or, if the court so orders, from the date of the application.

My husband got a decree for restitution of conjugal rights. Does maintenance stop?

Not by that decree alone. The Supreme Court so held in Rina Kumari v. Dinesh Kumar Mahto (2025).
