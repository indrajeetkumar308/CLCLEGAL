MSME & SME IPO Legal Advisory

Legal preparation for growing enterprises raising capital through an SME IPO on BSE SME or NSE Emerge.

Overview

The Practice

An SME IPO lets a small or medium enterprise raise equity from the public and list on a dedicated SME platform. Before that can happen, the company’s legal house must be in order: its constitution, title to its assets, licences, contracts, litigation and past filings are all examined and disclosed in the offer document.

We act as legal advisors on the Indian law side, working alongside the SEBI-registered merchant banker, statutory auditor and company secretary. Our role is to find and fix legal gaps early, so that they do not delay the issue or appear as risk factors in the prospectus.

The listing journey

From Private Company to Listed Enterprise

The usual sequence for an SME IPO. Timelines depend on the company’s state of readiness.

1

Readiness assessment

Review of financials, eligibility, corporate records and pending litigation.

2

Conversion & restructuring

The company is converted into a public limited company and its constitution and capital are aligned for listing.

3

Appointment of intermediaries

Merchant banker, registrar, auditor and legal advisor are appointed.

4

Legal due diligence

Title, licences, contracts, litigation and past filings are verified and gaps are cured.

5

Draft offer document

The DRHP is prepared with legal disclosures and filed with the stock exchange.

6

Exchange approval & filing

Observations are answered, in-principle approval is obtained and the prospectus is filed with the Registrar of Companies.

✓

Issue & listing

The issue opens, shares are allotted and trading begins on the SME platform.

Eligibility conditions such as track record, profitability and net worth are prescribed by SEBI and the exchanges and are revised from time to time. They are checked afresh for every issue.

Frequently Asked Questions

What is an SME IPO?

A public issue by a small or medium enterprise that lists on the SME platform of a stock exchange, such as BSE SME or NSE Emerge, under the SME chapter of the SEBI ICDR Regulations.

Can a private limited company go for an IPO?

It must first convert into a public limited company. The conversion, and the changes to its articles and capital, are part of the pre-IPO legal work.

Is a merchant banker required?

Yes. An SME IPO must be managed by a SEBI-registered merchant banker. The chambers works with the banker as legal advisor on the issue.

Why is legal due diligence so important?

Every material legal fact must be disclosed in the offer document. Defects in title, missing licences or undisclosed litigation can delay approval or expose the company and its directors to liability.

Are eligibility rules fixed?

No. SEBI and the exchanges revise eligibility conditions from time to time, so they are checked against the current rules at the start of each engagement.
